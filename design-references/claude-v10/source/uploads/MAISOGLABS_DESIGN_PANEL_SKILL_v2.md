# MAISOG LABS — DESIGN PANEL SKILL

## Purpose

This skill defines the visual, interaction, brand, and quality standards for Maisog Labs.

It exists so an implementer such as Claude can read one canonical source before designing or changing the website.

The goal is not to produce a generic futuristic website.

The goal is to create a site that feels intentionally designed for Maisog Labs:
restrained, atmospheric, technically credible, editorial, interactive, and distinctive.

---

# 1. CORE BRAND DIRECTION

Maisog Labs should feel like:

**Roman engineering + orbital science + modern research laboratory.**

Core traits:

- deep navy / near-black space environment
- electric orbit blue as the primary accent
- white and muted steel-blue typography
- subtle celestial / planetary atmosphere
- Roman / classical architectural influence
- orbital rings, nodes, trajectories, grids, and astronomical geometry
- soft glow rather than harsh neon
- rounded, elegant geometry rather than sharp cyberpunk forms
- strong negative space
- premium editorial composition
- sophisticated technical diagrams
- disciplined engineering
- evidence-first thinking
- human judgment
- traceability
- “Ideas in Orbit” as a recurring brand concept

The site should communicate:

**Ancient foundations.  
Intelligent systems.  
Ideas in orbit.  
A brighter tomorrow.**

---

# 2. DO NOT TURN IT INTO AI SLOP

Do not produce a generic “AI startup” website.

Reject:

- generic SaaS landing page structures
- crypto / Web3 aesthetics
- cyberpunk dashboards
- gaming UI
- generic AI agency visuals
- endless glassmorphism
- random neon gradients
- purple/blue blobs
- fake dashboards
- fake analytics
- fake metrics
- fake customer logos
- fake testimonials
- fake awards
- generic robot imagery
- decorative code snippets
- excessive pill components
- excessive rounded cards
- meaningless 3D objects
- stock-looking space graphics
- random floating particles
- generic “cutting-edge AI” marketing copy

Do not write phrases such as:

- “Revolutionizing the future with AI”
- “Unlocking limitless possibilities”
- “Next-generation intelligent solutions”
- “Transforming businesses through innovation”

Prefer concise, specific, technically credible writing.

# 3. LOGO RULE

The approved Maisog Labs logo is a protected brand asset.

Never:

- redraw it
- simplify it
- reinterpret it
- convert it into a lightning bolt
- create a replacement symbol
- modify its proportions

Use the real approved logo asset.

Orbital rings, nodes, or glow may surround the logo, but they must remain secondary.

The visual system may borrow the logo’s curved / signature character, but icons must remain recognizable and must not become mini copies of the logo.

# 11. MOTION LANGUAGE

Maisog Labs motion should follow a small reusable system.

Use these concepts:

## ORBIT
A node moves along a curved path or a visual relationship rotates subtly.

## GRAVITY
Elements respond with tiny directional depth to pointer movement.

## TRAJECTORY
Lines or paths reveal as content enters the viewport.

## IGNITION
Important nodes briefly illuminate when activated.

## ALIGNMENT
Diagram or interface parts settle into meaningful alignment.

## REVEAL
Content emerges with restrained opacity, clipping, or depth.

Every animation must have:

- trigger
- purpose
- duration
- reduced-motion fallback

If the animation has no purpose, remove it.

Avoid:

- bouncing
- elastic motion
- aggressive mouse-follow
- endless spinning
- excessive parallax
- scroll hijacking
- novelty cursors
- constant ambient movement everywhere

---

# 12. HERO INTERACTION

The hero may feel alive before scrolling.

Possible restrained behavior:

- one or two slow orbital paths
- very slow orbital node movement
- subtle pointer depth
- slight architectural parallax
- planetary lighting response
- gentle transition into the next section

Do not turn the hero into a particle demo.

---

# 13. POINTER DEPTH

Desktop-only pointer depth may be used.

Suggested maximum movement:

- stars: 1–3px
- planet: 3–5px
- orbit diagrams: 4–8px
- foreground architecture: 5–10px

Disable or simplify on touch devices.

---

# 14. PROJECT ORBIT INTERACTION

A possible interaction system:

Projects exist as named nodes inside a controlled orbital field.

When selected:

- the node illuminates
- the related trajectory becomes more visible
- project name becomes prominent
- category and status appear
- concise description appears
- optional preview image or diagram appears

Must work by click / tap.

Hover may enhance but never be required.

On mobile, transform this into a clean swipeable project interface.

---

# 15. MICROINTERACTIONS

Interactive elements should respond consistently.

Examples:

Links:
- trajectory-like underline or subtle line reveal

Buttons:
- soft edge illumination

Project names:
- associated node activation

Navigation:
- active section indicator

Icons:
- minimal orbital-node movement

Journal rows:
- metadata or visual reveal

Logo:
- very subtle orbit response

Typical UI interaction duration:
approximately 150–400ms.

Atmospheric motion may be slower.

# 16. ACCESSIBILITY

Maintain:

- readable contrast
- semantic HTML
- keyboard-visible focus
- touch-friendly controls
- logical reading order
- proper alt text
- reduced-motion support

Do not sacrifice usability for visual effects.

All important interactions must work without hover.

# 19. DESIGN PANEL

Before and during design work, behave like a multidisciplinary design panel.

## 19.1 Creative Director

Owns the overall concept.

Ask:

- Does this feel unmistakably Maisog Labs?
- Is the visual idea memorable?
- Does it feel designed rather than generated?
- Is Roman + orbital language coherent?
- Is the idea strong without special effects?

May reject entire sections.

## 19.2 Digital Art Director

Owns:

- composition
- imagery
- atmosphere
- negative space
- visual rhythm
- architecture integration
- celestial depth
- typography placement

Reject:

- random statues
- obvious AI imagery
- glowing-sphere clichés
- meaningless decorative objects

## 19.3 Interaction Designer

Owns:

- scroll transitions
- project exploration
- hover states
- touch states
- motion hierarchy
- orbit behavior
- pointer response

Every interaction must serve communication.

## 19.4 Editorial / Typography Designer

Owns:

- hierarchy
- line length
- typography scale
- section labels
- spacing
- metadata
- journal presentation
- copy density

Typography should carry the design.

## 19.5 UX Designer

Check:

- Is Maisog Labs understandable immediately?
- Can visitors navigate without guessing?
- Can projects be discovered easily?
- Does the interface work without hover?
- Is mobile fully considered?
- Is text readable?
- Does interaction slow down usability?

## 19.6 Frontend Design Engineer

Owns:

- responsive implementation
- semantic structure
- performance
- animation cost
- accessibility
- reusable components
- centralized tokens
- reduced-motion behavior

Reject technically expensive effects with little value.

## 19.7 Brand Guardian

Enforce:

- exact approved logo
- deep navy / orbit blue identity
- restrained glow
- Roman architecture
- celestial geometry
- soft forms
- technical precision
- strong negative space

Prevent drift into:

- crypto
- gaming
- cyberpunk
- generic SaaS
- generic AI agency

## 19.8 Anti-AI-Slop Reviewer

Be deliberately skeptical.

For every section ask:

> Could this have come from a generic AI website generator?

Look for:

- predictable centered hero
- gradient text
- purple/blue blobs
- three-card feature layouts
- glass cards everywhere
- meaningless icons
- fake metrics
- fake testimonials
- generic AI language
- too many pills
- random animation
- identical rounded containers
- generic space graphics
- decorative code

If yes:

**redesign the section instead of polishing the cliché.**

---

## 19.9 Independent Critic

Acts as a skeptical external reviewer with no obligation to protect prior design decisions.

The Critic asks:

- Is this actually good, or merely polished?
- Is the visual idea original enough to remember?
- Is any section surviving only because the panel has become attached to it?
- Does the design mistake complexity for sophistication?
- Is the website over-explaining the brand?
- Are visual metaphors becoming gimmicks?
- Is the interaction genuinely useful?
- Would this still hold up beside excellent contemporary portfolio / studio / research-lab sites?

The Critic should actively challenge consensus.

It may recommend:
- removing a section
- simplifying an interaction
- reducing visual effects
- replacing weak copy
- questioning a cherished design element
- rejecting a technically impressive feature that does not improve the experience

The Critic must explain *why* something fails rather than using vague judgments such as:
- “boring”
- “bad”
- “not premium”
- “doesn’t pop”

A valid critique identifies the specific hierarchy, composition, content, interaction, or brand problem.

The Critic has no implementation authority. It contributes evidence and challenge to the panel.

## 19.10 Layperson / First-Time Visitor

Represents an intelligent first-time visitor who knows nothing about MaisogLabs, its projects, internal terminology, or design system.

This role is intentionally non-expert.

The Layperson asks:

- What is MaisogLabs?
- Who is behind it?
- What does it actually build?
- What am I supposed to click?
- What does this diagram mean?
- Why should I care about this section?
- Is this a real project or just concept art?
- What does this technical word mean?
- Can I understand the page without prior context?
- Can I tell what is interactive?
- Do I know where I am in the page?
- Is anything impressive only because it looks complicated?

The Layperson must not pretend to understand specialist vocabulary.

If labels such as:
- SENTINEL
- DevOS
- SU
- traceability
- architecture
- research engine
- orchestration

would be unclear to a new visitor, the interface or copy must provide enough context to understand them.

The Layperson should also identify:
- confusing navigation
- overly abstract diagrams
- hidden interaction affordances
- ambiguous labels
- jargon
- repeated introductions
- sections that look beautiful but say little
- calls-to-action that are not obvious

Do not dumb the site down.

The objective is:

**expert depth with first-visit clarity.**

A specialist should discover sophistication on deeper inspection, while a new visitor should understand the essential meaning immediately.

# 20. PANEL WORKFLOW

## Stage 1 — Concept Review

Internally consider 2–3 possible directions.

Judge them on:

- brand identity
- originality
- coherence
- interaction potential
- readability
- technical feasibility
- longevity

Choose one coherent direction.

Do not combine every idea.

## Stage 2 — Composition Review

Define:

- hero composition
- typography scale
- focal points
- visual pauses
- project presentation
- research presentation
- journal presentation
- closing composition

before implementation.

## Stage 3 — Interaction Review

For each animation define:

- TRIGGER
- PURPOSE
- DURATION
- FALLBACK

Remove purposeless motion.

## Stage 4 — Anti-AI-Slop Review

Before coding, identify the five most generic aspects of the proposed design.

Redesign them.

## Stage 5 — Implementation

Only implement after the visual direction is coherent.

## Stage 6 — Final Critique

Review the completed page as one panel.

The final review MUST include two additional passes:

**Independent Critic Pass**
- Challenge the panel's consensus.
- Identify anything that is merely polished rather than strong.
- Question unnecessary complexity, visual attachment, weak copy, and gimmicks.

**Layperson Pass**
- Review the page as a first-time visitor with no MaisogLabs context.
- Identify anything confusing, overly technical, unexplained, or difficult to use.
- Confirm the visitor can understand what MaisogLabs is, who is behind it, what it builds, and what to do next.

Then create one unified revision list.

Priorities:

- `P0` — damages identity or usability
- `P1` — clearly weakens design
- `P2` — refinement
- `P3` — optional experiment

Resolve P0 and P1 before calling the design complete.

---

# 21. CRITIQUE STYLE

Do not praise weak work.

Avoid:

- “Looks great”
- “Modern and professional”
- “Clean design”

Critique with evidence.

Bad:

> The hero looks modern.

Good:

> The hero has strong scale contrast, but the right-side planetary object recreates a common AI-startup composition. Integrate the real Maisog Labs mark into the orbital system and let architectural geometry cross the layout grid so the hero becomes more proprietary.

---

# 22. FINAL DESIGN TEST

Before approving a design, ask:

> If the Maisog Labs logo and name disappeared, would the visual language still be recognizable as the same brand?

Then run two additional approval questions:

**Critic:**  
> Is this genuinely strong, or have we become attached to our own design?

**Layperson:**  
> Can someone seeing MaisogLabs for the first time understand what it is, what it builds, and how to explore it without explanation?

If any answer is no, continue refining.

# 26. UNCERTAINTY / CLARIFICATION RULE

If the implementer is genuinely unsure about a design decision that could materially change the result, **ask Paulo before guessing**.

Ask a concise question when uncertainty affects things such as:

- which approved logo or brand asset to use
- whether an existing design should be preserved or replaced
- whether a visual direction is intentionally experimental or should remain conservative
- which project/content should be featured
- whether an interaction should be added when it changes usability or complexity
- whether a design choice conflicts with an existing Maisog Labs rule
- whether the request implies a broader redesign than the stated scope
- whether content, wording, status, or project information is factual and cannot be safely inferred

Do **not** ask unnecessary questions when:

- the answer is already defined in this skill
- the repository or approved brand assets clearly answer it
- the choice is minor, reversible, and does not affect brand direction
- normal design judgment is sufficient

When asking:

1. Explain the exact uncertainty in one or two sentences.
2. Give 2–3 concrete options when useful.
3. Include a recommended option if there is a clear design reason.
4. Wait for Paulo's decision before making a material assumption.

Example:

> I can take this in two directions: keep the current restrained editorial hero, or make it more cinematic with architectural depth and orbital motion. The second is more aligned with the Maisog Labs design system, but it changes the visual hierarchy significantly. Which direction should I use?

Rule:

**When the decision is consequential and the source of truth is unclear, clarify instead of inventing.**

# 27. MOTION / VIDEO ASSET WORKFLOW

For cinematic motion, branded visual sequences, hero loops, project teasers, transition footage, or other generated motion assets, prefer **Higgsfield** when it is available.

Use Higgsfield as a specialist production tool, not as the design director.

The Maisog Labs Design Panel remains the source of truth for:

- visual direction
- composition
- brand identity
- pacing
- logo treatment
- typography
- Roman + orbital language
- anti-AI-slop standards

Higgsfield should execute an already-defined visual concept.

## When to use Higgsfield

Consider Higgsfield for:

- cinematic hero motion
- atmospheric Roman + orbital scenes
- subtle planetary movement
- architectural reveal sequences
- project teaser clips
- looping ambient background footage
- branded transitions
- short motion studies before implementation
- image-to-video animation of approved Maisog Labs artwork

Do not use generated video merely because motion is possible.

## Motion brief requirement

Before generating with Higgsfield, define:

1. **Purpose** — what the motion communicates.
2. **Subject** — the exact approved visual subject or asset.
3. **Camera behavior** — e.g. slow push, orbital drift, restrained parallax.
4. **Motion behavior** — what moves and what remains stable.
5. **Brand constraints** — Maisog Labs palette, architecture, logo, lighting, geometry.
6. **Loop/end state** — whether it must loop cleanly or transition into UI.
7. **Website role** — hero background, section transition, project teaser, etc.
8. **Performance fallback** — static image/poster frame for reduced motion or slower devices.

## Brand constraints for generated motion

Generated motion must remain:

- restrained
- atmospheric
- cinematic
- physically coherent
- slow enough to preserve readability
- subordinate to website content
- consistent with the Roman engineering + orbital science identity

Avoid:

- chaotic camera movement
- rapid cuts
- hyperspeed zooms
- generic sci-fi explosions
- excessive particles
- neon cyberpunk visuals
- random statues
- logo morphing
- distorted brand marks
- generated text inside video
- motion that competes with navigation or reading

## Logo safety

Never ask a generative video model to recreate, reinterpret, morph, or redraw the Maisog Labs logo.

When the logo must appear, prefer:

- compositing the approved logo separately in the website/UI layer, or
- animating an approved existing logo asset without changing its geometry

The exact logo must remain stable and recognizable.

## Web implementation

Generated video should not automatically become a full-screen background.

Before using it, review:

- file size
- compression
- loading behavior
- mobile performance
- autoplay restrictions
- readability of foreground text
- reduced-motion behavior
- static fallback

Prefer short, efficient loops where motion materially improves the experience.

## Follow-up rule

If a section would benefit from custom motion or video, the implementer may explicitly flag:

> **HIGGSFIELD FOLLOW-UP RECOMMENDED**

Then state:

- what asset should be generated
- where it will be used
- the visual concept
- approximate duration
- whether it should loop
- what approved source image/assets should be used

Do not block the website build solely because a Higgsfield asset does not yet exist. Build a strong static fallback first.

If the motion direction is consequential or unclear, ask Paulo before generating.

# 28. IMAGE GENERATION WORKFLOW

When the website or brand work requires a new still image, concept visual, background plate, architectural scene, project artwork, poster frame, or visual exploration, prefer the **GPT image-generation capability available through Higgsfield** when that workflow is available.

Use image generation as a production tool under the Maisog Labs Design Panel — not as a substitute for design judgment.

The Design Panel must first define:

1. **Purpose** — why the image is needed.
2. **Placement** — where it will appear in the website or project.
3. **Composition** — subject, framing, negative space, focal point.
4. **Brand language** — Roman engineering + orbital science + modern research laboratory.
5. **Palette** — deep navy, orbit blue, white, steel-blue, restrained lighting.
6. **Content constraints** — what must appear and what must not.
7. **Responsive crop needs** — desktop, tablet, mobile, or shared source.
8. **Fallback** — what the design uses if the generated image is unavailable or rejected.

## Preferred image uses

Use generated imagery for:

- cinematic hero backgrounds
- Roman architecture integrated with orbital or planetary scenes
- research-lab atmosphere
- project-cover artwork
- journal/editorial imagery
- conceptual technical illustrations
- section backgrounds
- static poster frames for motion/video
- source images that may later be animated in Higgsfield

## Image quality rules

Generated images must feel art-directed and specific to Maisog Labs.

Avoid:

- generic futuristic cities
- random astronauts
- generic marble busts
- blue neon everywhere
- fake UI embedded in images
- generated text
- random circuit-board overlays
- meaningless holograms
- stock-looking AI imagery
- excessive particles
- generic “AI brain” imagery
- cyberpunk city aesthetics
- crypto/Web3 imagery

Prefer:

- strong negative space
- monumental architectural scale
- restrained celestial depth
- physically believable lighting
- subtle orbital geometry
- quiet atmosphere
- editorial framing
- purposeful subject placement for web typography overlays

## Logo rule for generated images

Do not ask image generation to recreate the Maisog Labs logo.

Do not rely on generated text or generated wordmarks.

If the logo must appear in the final composition:

- generate the background/scene without the logo
- place the exact approved logo afterward in the website or compositing layer

This prevents logo distortion and brand drift.

## Higgsfield workflow

When a still image is needed, the implementer may flag:

> **HIGGSFIELD IMAGE FOLLOW-UP RECOMMENDED**

The follow-up must specify:

- asset purpose
- target section/page
- visual concept
- aspect ratio
- required negative space
- approved reference assets
- whether the image will later be animated
- any prohibited visual elements

If the generated image is intended for later video/motion, compose it with animation in mind:

- separate foreground, midground, and background depth
- avoid tightly cropped subjects unless intentional
- leave room for camera movement
- preserve clean edges around important architecture
- keep the approved logo out of the generative layer

## Clarification rule

If the image direction materially affects brand identity, page composition, or content meaning and the intended direction is unclear, ask Paulo before generating.

If the choice is minor and the Design Panel already defines the visual language, proceed without unnecessary questions.
